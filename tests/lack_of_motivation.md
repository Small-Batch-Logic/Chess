# Lack of Motivation: The "Why" Problem
**Date:** 2026-05-12
**Subject:** The Absence of Player Agency and Narrative Stakes

The question "What am I trying to accomplish?" highlights the lack of **motivation** in the current game design. 

## 1. The "Wandering" Problem
Currently, both the Player and the System are just wandering the map. 
- **The System** has no destination. It isn't trying to deliver passengers, reach a terminal, or escape the city. It is just a random dot.
- **The Player** has no mission. You aren't "policing" the lines, "repairing" a broken network, or "racing" to a destination.

## 2. No "Game Over" for the AI
In most games, the enemy is trying to achieve something that you must stop. 
- Because the System is just moving randomly forever, there is no **urgency**. 
- You can take 100 turns to "intercept" it, and nothing changes. This makes the "accomplishment" feel hollow.

## 3. The "Chess" Misnomer
Chess is a game of **elimination**. You accomplish the removal of the opponent's pieces until the King is trapped.
- In **Board Control**, nothing is eliminated. The board just gets "messier" with blue and red lines until they touch. 
- You aren't "clearing" the board or "winning" territory.

---

### How to give the player an "Accomplishment":
To make the player feel like they've actually achieved something, the game needs a **Dynamic Objective**:

1. **The "Heist" Variant:** The System is trying to reach a specific "High Value" stop (e.g., The Airport or City Hall). Your accomplishment is **preventing** it from getting there by intercepting its path.
2. **The "Network Repair" Variant:** There are "Broken" stops on the map. Your accomplishment is visiting all of them before the System "Ghost" intercepts you.
3. **The "Territory War" Variant:** You and the System are competing to "claim" the most stops. Your accomplishment is owning 60% of the city's network.

**Conclusion:**
Right now, you are accomplishing a **collision**. To feel like a game, you need to be accomplishing a **victory over an objective**.
